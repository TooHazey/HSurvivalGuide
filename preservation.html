Food Preservation

Preserving food multiplies the value of what you grow or gather. The USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning is the definitive free reference.

Main Methods

  • Canning — Pressure canning required for low-acid foods (vegetables, meats). Water-bath for high-acid foods (fruits, pickles, jams). Follow tested recipes only.
  • Drying / Dehydrating — Removes moisture so bacteria cannot grow. Works well for fruits, vegetables, herbs, and jerky.
  • Freezing — Excellent quality retention when power is available. Blanch vegetables first.
  • Fermentation — Sauerkraut, pickles, and other lactic-acid ferments. Requires salt and anaerobic conditions.
  • Salting & Smoking — Traditional methods for meat and fish.

Critical Safety Rules

  • Never invent canning recipes — botulism risk is real
  • Use only tested processing times from USDA or Ball guides
  • Adjust for altitude
  • Inspect jars for seal failure before eating
  • When in doubt, throw it out
