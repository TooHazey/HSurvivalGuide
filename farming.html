Farming & Food Production

Long-term survival depends on growing food. Start with calorie-dense, reliable crops and expand over time.

Priority Crops (High Calories, Relatively Easy)

  • Potatoes & sweet potatoes — High yield, store well, grow in many climates
  • Beans & peas — Protein, nitrogen-fixing, dry for storage
  • Corn — Calories and animal feed; pair with beans
  • Squash & pumpkins — High yield, long storage (winter squash)
  • Grains — Wheat, oats, or rice depending on climate (more labor-intensive)

Fast Supporting Crops

  • Leafy greens (lettuce, spinach, kale, chard)
  • Radishes, carrots, beets
  • Herbs for flavor and some medicinal use
  • Onions and garlic (storage and pest resistance)

Basic Principles

  • Soil — Add organic matter (compost, manure). Avoid tilling excessively once established.
  • Water — Consistent moisture is critical. Mulch heavily to retain water.
  • Sun — Most vegetables need 6+ hours of direct sun.
  • Seed saving — Grow open-pollinated varieties so you can save seed year to year.
  • Succession planting — Stagger plantings for continuous harvest.

Small Livestock (Optional but High Value)

  • Chickens — Eggs and meat; relatively easy
  • Rabbits — Efficient meat production
  • Goats or sheep — Milk and meat where forage is available

Start with plants first. Animals require daily care and feed.

Primary sources: USDA publications and university extension guides.
