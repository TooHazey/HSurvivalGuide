Trades & Practical Skills

Skills that let you produce or repair essential items become highly valuable over time.

High-Value Skills

  • Blacksmithing / Metalworking — Making and repairing tools, hinges, nails. Requires a forge (can be improvised) and basic hammer skills.
  • Woodworking — Building shelters, furniture, tool handles, and containers. Hand tools (saw, chisel, plane, drill) are sufficient.
  • Leatherworking — Repairing boots, making sheaths, bags, and straps. Basic stitching and edge finishing.
  • Soap & candle making — Hygiene and light from fats and ash (lye) or beeswax.
  • Fiber skills — Spinning, weaving, or knitting for clothing and cordage.
  • Basic mechanics — Keeping engines, pumps, or generators running if fuel is available.

Where to Start

  1. Master hand-tool woodworking and basic metal repair first
  2. Learn to make and maintain your own tools
  3. Practice with scrap materials before working on critical items
  4. Document processes so knowledge isn't lost

Knowledge Sources

Many older trade manuals and USDA/Extension publications cover practical skills. Search Army field manuals for field expedients and look for public-domain woodworking and blacksmithing texts.
