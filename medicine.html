Medicine & First Aid (Military / Austere Style)

Prioritize life-threatening issues using military field medicine principles from U.S. Army doctrine (ATP 3-50.21, ATP 4-02.11 / TCCC concepts). This is not a substitute for training or professional care.

Serious injuries require professional medical care whenever possible. These are emergency principles only.

MARCH-PAWS Priorities

Treat in this order. Massive bleeding is the top priority.

  • Massive Hemorrhage — Control life-threatening bleeding immediately. Direct pressure first. For limb bleeding that does not stop, apply a tourniquet high and tight. Note the time.
  • Airway — Ensure the airway is open. Head-tilt/chin-lift or recovery position if no spinal concern.
  • Respiration — Check breathing. Seal open chest wounds (occlusive dressing). Assist ventilation if needed.
  • Circulation — Check pulse, treat for shock (keep warm, elevate legs if no fracture, monitor).
  • Hypothermia / Head injury — Prevent heat loss. Monitor for head trauma.
  • Pain — Manage pain if supplies allow (after life threats are controlled).
  • Antibiotics — Prevent infection if available and indicated.
  • Wounds — Clean and cover once stable.
  • Splinting — Immobilize fractures and dislocations.

Tourniquet illustration (public domain). Apply high and tight on the limb, note the time of application.

Tourniquet Basics

  • Place 2–3 inches above the wound (or high on the limb if exact location is unclear).
  • Tighten until bleeding stops and distal pulse is gone.
  • Write the time of application on the casualty or the tourniquet itself.
  • Do not loosen in the field unless trained and conditions allow.
  • Improvised tourniquets (cloth + stick windlass) can work but commercial ones are more reliable.

Common Field Issues

  • Wound care — Irrigate with clean water, remove debris, cover with clean dressing. Watch for infection signs.
  • Burns — Cool with clean water 10–20 minutes, cover with clean non-stick material. Do not break blisters.
  • Hypothermia — Remove wet clothes, insulate from ground, warm the core gradually.
  • Sprains/fractures — RICE (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation) for soft tissue. Immobilize fractures before moving.
  • Dehydration / diarrhea — Oral rehydration solution (clean water + salt + sugar).

Prevention & Resources

  • Hand hygiene and clean water prevent most illness
  • Carry a basic IFAK (Individual First Aid Kit) with tourniquet, pressure bandage, chest seal if possible
  • Primary sources: ATP 3-50.21 Survival and ATP 4-02.11 Casualty Response (public domain Army publications)
