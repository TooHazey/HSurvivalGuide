Medicine & First Aid

In austere conditions, focus on preventing problems and treating the most common life-threatening issues. This is not a substitute for medical training.

Serious injuries and illnesses require professional care whenever possible.

Immediate Priorities (MARCH)

  • Massive hemorrhage — Direct pressure, tourniquets for limb bleeding
  • Airway — Ensure the person can breathe
  • Respiration — Chest injuries, breathing rate
  • Circulation — Shock prevention, keep warm
  • Hypothermia / Head injury — Protect from cold, monitor

Common Issues

  • Wound care — Clean thoroughly, cover, watch for infection (redness, heat, pus, fever)
  • Dehydration & diarrhea — Oral rehydration (clean water + salt + sugar)
  • Burns — Cool with clean water, cover, do not pop blisters
  • Hypothermia — Remove wet clothing, insulate, warm gradually
  • Sprains & fractures — Immobilize, elevate, ice if available

Prevention

  • Hand hygiene and clean water
  • Proper wound cleaning
  • Adequate calories and rest
  • Shelter from extreme temperatures

Primary sources: U.S. Army first aid manuals (FM 4-25.11) and related field medical guidance.
