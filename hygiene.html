Hygiene & Sanitation

Disease from poor sanitation kills more people in prolonged emergencies than trauma or starvation. Prevention is far easier than treatment.

Core Practices

  • Hand washing — Soap and clean water (or ash/sand if soap is unavailable) after toilet use and before eating or handling food.
  • Waste disposal — Bury human waste at least 200 feet from water sources and living areas. Use a latrine or cat-hole system.
  • Water separation — Keep drinking water, washing water, and waste completely separate.
  • Food safety — Cook thoroughly, avoid cross-contamination, and store food protected from pests.

Personal Hygiene

  • Wash regularly when water allows
  • Keep clothing as clean and dry as possible
  • Treat minor cuts and scrapes promptly
  • Manage waste and standing water to reduce insects

Making Soap (Basic)

Traditional soap can be made from wood ash (for lye) and animal fat or plant oils. This requires care — lye is caustic. Modern references and older public-domain household manuals describe the process.

Primary sources: U.S. Army field sanitation guidance and public health principles.
